Where a live session goes

The strip is already full: quota chips on the left of the notch, tokens and spend on the right. Sessions are a different axis — not how much is left, but what is running and which one wants you back. These are three answers to that conflict, at real size. Judge them at 100%; the magnified views are for detail only.

Chosen: C · Baton, with two changes to the variant as drawn. A +N count on the lozenge, which answers the cost recorded against C below: two sessions waiting at once no longer look like one. And the panel's Running list is gone — the panel shows only what is asking for you. Shown as bare project names it read as a list of folders or dependencies rather than agents at work, and it asked nothing of the reader: what is merely alive is already said by the chips beside the notch. A and B are kept here as the record of what was weighed.

A

Inhabit

Sessions take no new width at all. The chip you already have grows a state rule underneath it — moving for working, steady red for waiting. An agent with no session looks exactly as it does today.

B

Roster

Sessions get their own real estate: a count with one dot per state, sitting to the right of the notch. Honest about how many there are — and it evicts the spend figure to pay for the room.

C

Baton

The strip stays exactly as it is until something actually needs you. Working and compacting are silent. One session goes to waiting and a named lozenge appears — the project, not the agent, because that is what tells you which window to go to.
